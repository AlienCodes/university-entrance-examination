Many people think birds have simple minds, but ravens (渡鸦) are a surprising exception. Their intelligence has long been compared to that of monkeys and even humans. One of the most surprising things they can do is to make plans for the future.

In one experiment, ravens were trained to use a tool to get food. Later, they were given a choice among several things, including the tool and other useless objects. Most of the ravens chose the tool, even with no food reward at once. This showed they could remember how to use the tools and plan ahead to get the treat later.

Ravens are good at solving real-life problems. They use sticks to dig insects out of trees, and some even drop nuts (坚果) on roads so cars can crack them open. They can solve problems that require several steps, like using a short stick to get a longer one, then using the long stick to reach food.

Their intelligence isn’t just about solving problems. Ravens have rich social lives. They remember different faces, including those of other ravens and humans. They can hold grudges against people who have treated them badly, and even teach their young to stay away from those people.

Sadly, many kinds of ravens are suffering from habitat (栖息地) loss and pollution. As we learn more about their intelligence, scientists hope people will see them as more than just “common birds” and take action to protect them.
