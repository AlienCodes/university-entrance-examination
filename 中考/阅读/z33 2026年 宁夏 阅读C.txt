Water pollution is a serious problem around the world, and it is difficult to deal with. Traditional water sensors (传感器) can only work in fixed places. As a result, they cannot cover large water bodies like rivers, lakes or oceans. To solve this problem, scientists created a bionic (仿生) tool—a robotic fish.

The robotic fish is a great example of bionics. It shows how scientists copy nature to solve problems. In fact, the idea of copying nature is not new. The Wright brothers created their first plane by studying the flight of birds. Modern scientists created robotic fish by studying the swimming skills of trout (鳟鱼). Trout are good at moving fast in water with very little energy. By “learning” the way trout move, robotic fish can work for ten hours after they are fully charged.

According to scientists, the robotic fish has a special soft tail, so it can swim quietly and smoothly under water. It will not scare water animals or harm the underwater environment. Because of the small sensors inside its “body”, it can “taste” water to find harmful things. Once the robotic fish finds water pollution, it will send a warning message to a central computer at once. The robotic fish can even “eat” small pieces of plastic in water bodies and repair itself when it gets broken. Now more and more robotic fish are being used in rivers, lakes and oceans all over the world. They work together as a team to reduce water pollution.

By copying nature, scientists have offered lots of possibilities for protecting the environment and improving people’s lives. As one scientist said, “Nature is our best teacher. This invention shows people’s creativity and the value of learning from nature.”
